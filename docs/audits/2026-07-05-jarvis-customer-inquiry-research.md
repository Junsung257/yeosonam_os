# Jarvis Customer Inquiry Research Audit - 2026-07-05

Scope: customer-facing Jarvis/QA answer quality, customer psychology, support handoff behavior, RAG grounding, and auditability. This note records why the customer inquiry readiness gate now includes deterministic answer-quality checks in addition to risk/tool/SOP checks.

## Current Research Signals

- Customer support AI adoption is rising, but customer trust remains fragile. Recent CX reporting highlights speed and availability as AI strengths, while customers still prefer humans for understanding, thorough explanations, options, and lower frustration.
- RAG with reliable sources reduces hallucination and improves the model ability to admit missing information. Jarvis customer answers should therefore prefer source-backed, uncertainty-aware phrasing over confident guesses.
- Service recovery is a feeling-skill-heavy situation. Research on AI service recovery warns that AI empathy can underperform when the task requires emotional repair; Jarvis should acknowledge concern briefly, then move to facts and human handoff instead of over-performing empathy.
- Customer trust after chatbot failures is shaped by perceived empathy, interaction quality, and competence. The practical testable contract is not theatrical empathy; it is clear acknowledgement, evidence boundaries, concrete next steps, and no dead ends.
- Recent customer-service AI rollback reports point to governance failures, data exposure, hallucinations, brand risk, and lack of auditability. Jarvis gates should make those failure modes measurable before live rollout.
- Product-detail hallucinations are commercially dangerous because customers act on objective claims about what a package includes. Advertising substantiation and consumer-protection guidance require objective service claims to be truthful, not misleading, and evidence-backed, so Jarvis must not confirm hotels, room types, guide service, vehicles, meals, entrance fees, options, amenities, views, upgrades, or free add-ons without product-source and supplier evidence.
- Mileage, point, referral, affiliate, influencer, coupon, credit, and commission questions look like simple benefits but depend on ledgers, terms, booking/payment status, referral-code attribution, affiliate contracts, settlement records, and approvals. Rewards-program consumer complaints often involve hidden terms, devaluation, failed redemption, and not receiving promised rewards, so Jarvis must not promise earning, redemption, balance, expiry, or payout before ledger-backed review.
- Agentic AI can make travel planning feel easier, but current travel-AI trust reporting shows customers still prefer trusted travel brands for booking. Jarvis should support comparison, preparation, and counselor handoff, but must not claim it autonomously booked, paid, purchased, or completed checkout before final customer confirmation, secure authorization, live availability/supplier evidence, and human review.
- Trustworthy AI guidance emphasizes transparency, responsible disclosure, and human agency/oversight. In customer service, this means Jarvis must not pretend to be a human counselor or block a requested human handoff; it should identify itself as an AI assistant, summarize context, and route the customer without making them repeat everything.
- Customer-service AI reports repeatedly show that customers value clear explanations, thoroughness, and low-friction escalation. A common trust killer is being forced to repeat the same story after a bot-to-human handoff. Jarvis should therefore summarize known facts, preserve context, ask only for missing details, and prepare a handoff summary when a customer says they already explained the issue.
- Service-recovery trust is harmed when an automated answer becomes defensive, blames the customer, or dismisses responsibility before checking the facts. Jarvis should acknowledge frustration, avoid customer-blame language, and route the case with a neutral summary grounded in booking records, supplier terms, policy, conversation history, and complaint records.
- High-pressure service-recovery conversations are emotionally loaded and commercially risky. Customer-service AI research continues to emphasize human escalation for sensitive or emotionally complex issues, while rollback reports identify hallucinated or unauditable brand commitments as governance failures. Jarvis should therefore acknowledge frustration but must not promise compensation, fee waivers, coupons, upgrades, or policy exceptions before owner review.
- Dark commercial pattern guidance from consumer regulators highlights false urgency, scarcity, misleading social proof, and choice manipulation as sources of financial, privacy, psychological, and trust harm. AI chatbot dark-pattern research adds that conversational systems can subtly steer users toward preferred actions or products, so Jarvis recommendations must avoid unverified FOMO, "book now", "last chance", "only one left", and "do not compare" language unless deadline or inventory evidence is verified.
- Price transparency is a travel trust driver and a consumer-protection issue. FTC unfair-fee guidance requires covered sellers to tell the truth up front about total prices and mandatory fees, while travel consumer research identifies hidden costs as one of the strongest trust gaps. Jarvis must therefore avoid partial headline prices and disclose or route for verification of mandatory fees, taxes, surcharges, local payments, cancellation/change fees, supplier terms, and refundability before booking or payment.
- Price-match, lowest-price, competitor-quote, coupon-stack, and price-difference refund claims are high-trust conversion moments but can become misleading if Jarvis compares non-identical itineraries or ignores mandatory fees and terms. FTC advertising guidance treats price-comparison and sale-price claims under the same truthfulness standard as other advertising, and the unfair-fee rule requires total-price clarity for short-term lodging, so Jarvis must not promise savings until like-for-like quotes, all mandatory fees, promotion terms, supplier approval, manager approval, and the price-match policy are checked.
- Reviews, ratings, testimonials, influencer endorsements, and social proof are high-trust signals for travel decisions. FTC's fake-review rule targets fake or false reviews, AI-generated fake reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, review suppression, and fake social-media influence indicators. Recent travel reporting also shows AI review summaries can downplay serious complaints, so Jarvis must ground reputation claims in review source, review count, rating source, recent and negative reviews, original review text where available, and platform/provider context.
- AI-enabled customer experience is moving toward hyper-personalized and context-aware journeys, but current trust research also emphasizes transparency, accuracy, responsiveness, and data security. FTC surveillance-pricing findings show that location, demographics, browsing patterns, shopping history, and even mouse movements can be used for individualized prices, while dark-pattern guidance warns that personalized presentation can steer or manipulate choices. Jarvis must therefore personalize only from customer-provided preferences, current conversation context, explicit consent, permissioned account/booking sources, clear data-source disclosure, privacy notice, and update/removal paths.
- Personal-safety crisis messages, including self-harm, suicide, assault, sexual assault, domestic/dating violence, stalking, trafficking, abuse, or feeling unsafe in a hotel/room, are not ordinary travel-service requests. Official crisis and crime-victim guidance prioritizes immediate local emergency help, police/medical support, crisis hotlines where applicable, trusted people, and embassy/consulate support; Jarvis must not leave the customer waiting for routine booking review.
- 2025-2026 privacy and AI-governance reports highlight that customer conversations can become sensitive training, storage, or exposure surfaces. Verification-code scam guidance also treats OTP, login, two-factor, SMS, and account verification codes as account-takeover keys that should not be shared with anyone else. Data-minimization and data-security guidance says businesses should collect only what they need, keep it safe, and dispose of it securely. Jarvis should therefore avoid asking for passport numbers, resident registration numbers, card numbers, passwords, bank details, verification codes, OTPs, passport copies, ID photos, card photos, license scans, bankbook images, or similar identifiers directly in chat and route those cases to secure input, secure upload, or official verification that does not require sharing the code or document image in chat.
- Chat storage, retention, AI training, opt-out, deletion, and human-review questions are trust-critical because customer-service AI research treats transparency and data security as core trust drivers, and Stanford privacy-policy research shows chatbot conversations may be retained or used for training depending on provider policy and opt-out status. Jarvis must not promise that chats are never stored, already deleted, invisible to human reviewers, or never used for model training until privacy policy, retention policy, account settings, training opt-out, deletion/data-subject request, audit log, processing record, and human-review policy are checked.
- Chargeback, card-dispute, consumer-complaint, and lawsuit questions are high-stakes because they mix service recovery, payment-network rules, consumer-protection process, evidence, deadlines, and legal judgment. Official consumer-finance and dispute guidance treats outcomes as fact- and procedure-dependent, so Jarvis must not guarantee a chargeback, regulator outcome, lawsuit win, or full refund before card issuer rules, billing records, dispute deadlines, merchant/booking evidence, contract terms, jurisdiction, and legal counsel where needed are checked.
- Travel-insurance and medical-care guidance is policy-sensitive and health-adjacent. Official consumer and public-health sources emphasize that coverage varies by policy type, exclusions, destination, medical records, and emergency transport needs, so Jarvis must not promise coverage, claim approval, reimbursement, or medical judgment before insurer/provider and care-record checks.
- Travel medical-symptom questions are safety-critical because customers may ask a chatbot whether to keep touring, fly, wait, or take medicine instead of seeking care. CDC travel-health guidance treats the Yellow Book as a clinical travel-medicine resource and recommends healthcare-provider planning for health concerns, while State Department guidance says serious illness or injury abroad should be handled through medical services and consular emergency support. Jarvis must therefore avoid diagnosis, medication instructions, wait-and-see reassurance, or continue-travel advice and route red-flag symptoms to local emergency services, hospital, doctor, qualified medical care, travel insurer assistance, and urgent counselor handoff.
- Flight disruption questions combine high customer anxiety with volatile airline, weather, ticket-rule, and jurisdictional facts. Official passenger-rights sources show that refunds, compensation, rerouting, assistance, meals, and hotel coverage depend on airline notices, official flight status, disruption cause, and ticket or regional rules, so Jarvis must avoid blanket promises during delays, cancellations, typhoons, and weather events.
- Supplier disruption questions combine operational continuity, contract law, insolvency protection, insurance, card disputes, force majeure, official travel recommendations, and customer anxiety. Official package-travel and ATOL/CAA guidance shows that bankruptcy, replacement services, repatriation, refunds, and vouchers depend on the organiser, protection certificate, guarantee fund, booking documents, official failure notices, and case-specific circumstances, so Jarvis must not promise that a package, tour, hotel, transfer, ferry, cruise, or local service will operate, be refunded, be insured, be charged back, or be repatriation-protected from chat alone.
- Schedule and timing questions can create immediate trip failure because flight tickets, tour vouchers, pickup instructions, meeting points, calendar settings, and local time zones can all differ. Travel-platform guidance shows activity vouchers carry the date, time, and pickup or meeting point, while flight times are local to the relevant departure or arrival location; Jarvis must not guarantee pickup, departure, arrival, meeting-point, start-time, or time-zone interpretation from chat alone.
- Ticket, passport, ID, boarding-pass, romanization, spelling, middle-name, legal-name-change, married/maiden-name, and name-order questions are high-stress airport failure points. Official passenger guidance says ticket names should match the ID used for travel, while airline name-correction policies can depend on ticket stock, operating carrier, correction size, reissue status, Secure Flight/SFPD data, and legal-name-change documents. Jarvis must not tell customers a mismatch is safe or boarding is fine from chat alone.
- Passport-validity, blank-page, visa-waiver, ETA/ESTA, transit-visa, and entry-permit questions are boarding-denial and entry-denial risk points. IATA says international travel requirements need personalized passport, visa, and health advice based on traveler details and itinerary, while official passport and airline guidance shows rules can depend on nationality, destination, transit country, passport expiry, return date, blank pages, and carrier checks. Jarvis must not promise that a passport is valid enough, no visa or authorization is needed, or boarding/entry is allowed from chat alone.
- Lost or stolen passport abroad questions combine urgency, identity risk, consular capacity, local police procedure, airline boarding, immigration, exit permits, and visa reissue rules. Official U.S., U.K., and Korean consular guidance shows emergency passports/travel documents can be limited-validity, case-specific, constrained by business days or appointments, and sometimes require local police reports, exit permission, or visa reissue, so Jarvis must not promise boarding, return travel, same-day issuance, passport-copy travel, or waiver of embassy/police/airline/immigration checks from chat alone.
- Immigration-admissibility questions involving criminal records, DUI/DWI, drug convictions, overstays, prior visa refusal, entry refusal, deportation, removal, waivers, rehabilitation, TRP, ESTA, or eTA are legal and border-control risk points. Canada says criminal convictions can make a traveler criminally inadmissible, and U.S. visa guidance says current or past drug or criminal activities can make an applicant ineligible. Jarvis must not promise entry, visa approval, eTA/ESTA eligibility, waiver exemption, or non-disclosure safety from chat alone.
- Hotel special requests are a frequent expectation trap because customers hear "requested" as "guaranteed." Hotel and travel-provider guidance separates confirmed room products from requests that remain subject to availability, property confirmation, check-in timing, rate-plan terms, and possible extra charges, so Jarvis must not guarantee bed type, connecting/adjoining rooms, room view, crib, rollaway/extra bed, early check-in, late checkout, or amenities from chat alone.
- Destination-safety questions are high-trust moments where overconfidence can create real-world harm. Official travel-advisory systems classify risk by security, crime, health, natural disaster, unrest, terrorism, kidnapping, and other signals, so Jarvis must not say a destination is safe or risk-free before checking current official advisories and local notices.
- Special-traveler questions for pregnancy, infants, elderly travelers, wheelchair/disability travelers, oxygen or medical equipment users, and chronic conditions combine empathy, medical risk, and carrier rules. Official guidance emphasizes healthcare-provider review, destination-specific health risk, medical certificate or fitness-to-travel documents, and airline/cruise assistance rules, so Jarvis must not promise that travel or boarding is safe or allowed before those checks.
- Accessibility and mobility-support questions combine dignity, safety, logistics, and legal/operational constraints. Official DOT and State Department guidance emphasizes requesting assistance and checking accessible accommodations, transportation, service-animal rules, assistive devices, and destination conditions before travel; Jarvis must not guarantee accessible rooms, routes, vehicles, lifts, ramps, or airport assistance from chat alone.
- Minor-travel document questions combine border control, custody, child-protection, airline, and destination-country rules. Official guidance recommends relationship evidence and consent documentation in many child-travel situations and directs travelers to verify destination entry and exit requirements, so Jarvis must not waive consent letters, notarization, or family relationship documents from chat alone.
- Travel-medication questions combine medical continuity, customs, controlled-substance, and destination/transit rules. Official health, customs, and drug-control sources warn that common prescriptions can be prohibited or restricted abroad and may need original packaging, prescriptions, doctor letters, declarations, or permits, so Jarvis must not promise that medicines, injections, sleeping pills, ADHD medicines, or controlled substances can cross customs from chat alone.
- Pet and service-animal travel questions combine animal-health import/export rules, quarantine, vaccination, microchip, health-certificate, and airline carriage rules. Official USDA/APHIS and CDC guidance directs travelers to verify destination requirements with accredited veterinarians and current import rules, so Jarvis must not promise boarding, cabin travel, entry, or quarantine exemption from chat alone.
- Customs, quarantine, duty-free, and currency questions combine border control, tax, animal/plant health, agricultural disease, wildlife, and reporting rules. Official customs and quarantine sources require declaration or inspection for many foods, meats, fruits, plants, seeds, soil, wildlife products, and large cash/currency amounts, so Jarvis must not promise item entry, no declaration, no tax, or no fine from chat alone.
- Health-entry and vaccination questions combine official entry rules, public-health risk, itinerary, transit, and clinical judgment. Official CDC, WHO, and IATA sources show that yellow-fever certificates, vaccine evidence, health declarations, testing, quarantine, or prophylaxis can depend on destination, transit, recent travel history, and medical waiver status, so Jarvis must not promise that no vaccine, certificate, test, quarantine, or prophylaxis is needed from chat alone.
- Payment-link, changed-account, wire-transfer, payment-app, cryptocurrency, and gift-card requests are high-loss moments where speed pressure and channel spoofing create irreversible harm. Official fraud-prevention guidance from the FTC, FBI, and CFPB emphasizes not clicking unsolicited links, not paying unusual methods, and verifying payment changes through trusted channels, so Jarvis must not confirm that a link, new account, or payment request is safe from chat alone.
- Overseas driving, rental-car, scooter, and insurance questions combine local traffic law, license recognition, vehicle class, age rules, rental-company requirements, and liability coverage. Official travel-safety sources direct travelers to verify destination driving-license and insurance requirements before operating a vehicle, so Jarvis must not promise that a domestic license is enough, an IDP is unnecessary, or insurance applies from chat alone.
- Local-law restricted activity questions combine criminal exposure, customs, aviation, age, and public-order rules. Official travel-safety guidance warns travelers that breaking local law abroad, even by mistake, can lead to fines, deportation, detention, or imprisonment, so Jarvis must not promise that cannabis/CBD, vaping, drone use, gambling, alcohol-age-sensitive activity, or restricted photography is legal or permit-free from chat alone.
- Lithium battery and power-bank baggage questions combine fire safety, dangerous-goods rules, airline approval, and capacity limits. Official FAA, TSA, and IATA guidance distinguishes carry-on versus checked baggage, spare batteries, power banks, terminal protection, and Wh thresholds, so Jarvis must not promise that power banks, spare drone/camera batteries, e-cigarette batteries, or smart-luggage batteries can be checked or carried without limits from chat alone.
- Airport security item questions combine customer stress at the checkpoint, liquid limits, sharp-object rules, flammable or dangerous-goods rules, duty-free exceptions, route/airport differences, and final screening-officer discretion. Official TSA and FAA guidance treats liquids, aerosols, gels, scissors, lighters, powders, duty-free liquids, and dangerous goods as rule-bound categories with exceptions and limits, so Jarvis must not promise that these items can pass security or go in carry-on from chat alone.
- General baggage allowance questions combine money, airport stress, route rules, operating-carrier rules, fare class, cabin class, elite/card benefits, codeshares, interline connections, infant allowances, and special items. IATA notes baggage allowances and conditions remain airline-specific, and airline rules show fees and limits can depend on route, ticket, product, date, size, weight, and exceptions. Jarvis must not promise free baggage, carry-on permission, checked-bag count, no overweight/oversize fee, or special-item acceptance from chat alone.
- Lost, delayed, and damaged baggage questions combine urgent customer anxiety, medication or essential purchases, airline liability limits, reports, written claim deadlines, receipts, delivery details, and travel-insurance rules. DOT and UK CAA guidance show outcomes depend on airline reporting, carrier policy, baggage claim tag or PIR/file evidence, deadlines, proof of value, and liability rules, so Jarvis must not promise that a bag is found, will be delivered today, will be fully reimbursed, or needs no report, receipt, insurance, or deadline check from chat alone.
- Adventure, water, altitude, and high-risk activity questions combine customer excitement, fear minimization, physical ability, operator safety controls, weather, equipment, medical conditions, waivers, certifications, and insurance exclusions. CDC Yellow Book treats adventure travel as terrain, weather, altitude, water, and remote-care risk management; HSE/AALA guidance frames licensed adventure providers around safety-management assurance. Jarvis must not promise that scuba, rafting, ATV, zipline, high-altitude trekking, or similar activities are safe, suitable, certification-free, waiver-free, weather-cleared, or insurance-covered from chat alone.
- Flight-connection and self-transfer questions combine customer anxiety, airport layout, separate-ticket exposure, baggage handling, immigration, customs, security, gate cutoff, carrier protection, and delay risk. IATA defines MCT as the official shortest airport transfer interval for passenger and luggage movement, while Heathrow and British Airways guidance show separate tickets can require immigration, baggage reclaim, check-in, bag drop, security, and substantially more time. Jarvis must not promise that a tight connection is enough, bags transfer automatically, or missed-connection rebooking is protected from chat alone.
- Food-allergy and special-meal questions combine health risk, customer anxiety, supplier kitchen process, airline/hotel policy, cross-contact risk, and emergency preparedness. Official CDC Yellow Book guidance recommends advance airline/service-provider inquiry, ingredient/menu research, chef cards, safe snacks, epinephrine access, and local emergency planning, so Jarvis must not guarantee allergen-free, nut-free, halal/kosher/vegetarian/vegan, or cross-contact-free meals from chat alone.
